What people use Multi Crop for

A few real jobs, with the settings that suit them — easier to picture than a feature list.

Detail shots for product listings

The most common one. You have front shots of twenty garments and want to show the fabric, a button, the label and the hem stitching for each.

  • Ratio 1:1, output fixed size 1000px
  • Crop count 4, file names {name}-d{nn}

Move four boxes into place, press Enter, next photo. After twenty garments you get eighty files, named in order from coat-d01.webp, in one zip. They are all the same size, so the listing layout doesn't jump around.

If a photo doesn't have the resolution and you draw a box too small, the tool warns you it will be scaled up. Make the box bigger, or export that one at original pixels.

Close-ups of paintings and prints

On an artwork page the brushstrokes, paper texture and signature matter as much as the full image. Here we'd keep original pixels, so the area comes out of the high-res scan untouched.

Even when the works themselves are different shapes, the detail shots look better in one ratio — usually 4:5 or 1:1.

Instagram grid in 3, 6 or 9 posts

Turn on Instagram feed split in Settings to cut one picture into 3, 6 or 9 tiles. Drag the block to frame it; each tile comes out at the feed ratio.

One catch: the number on each tile is the order to post. A profile grid shows the newest post top-left, so the bottom-right piece has to go up first. Files are named -post01 onward in that order — just post them by number.

Feed, story and thumbnail from one photo

Sometimes one event photo has to become an Instagram post (4:5), a story (9:16) and a YouTube thumbnail (16:9). Switch the ratio toggle to Selected crop and give each box its own ratio. One export gives you all three.

Property and interior photos

Pull the finishes, the light fittings and the view out of one wide living-room shot. Interior photos often lean a little, so straighten under Alignment first — the crops come out much cleaner.

Just cropping a pile of photos to one ratio

It doesn't have to be detail shots. If thirty photos all need to be 1:1, set the crop count to 1 and step through checking each position. For plain batch cropping it's quick enough.
