After one round you probably won't need it again. ---

One thing to keep in mind: the ratio and output settings are set once for the whole batch; the box positions are set per photo. The rest is just following that order.

1. Load your images

Drop image files onto the tool or press Choose images. JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and GIF work, up to 200 files and 200 MB each. Nothing leaves your computer, so there is no upload to wait for.

If you just want a feel for it, press Try the sample. The How it works button at the top walks you through the screen one step at a time.

2. Pick a ratio

Choose the crop ratio under Settings: 1:1, 4:5, 2:3, 3:4, 5:7, 16:9 or 9:16, or type your own under Custom. With a ratio locked, every crop on every image comes out the same shape.

Sometimes one image needs more than one shape. Switch the toggle above the ratio buttons from Whole batch to Selected crop and only the box you have selected changes — handy for pulling a 4:5 feed post and a 16:9 banner out of the same photo.

3. Set the crop count and place the boxes

Decide how many crops each image gets — up to six, or 0 to skip it. Once the boxes appear:

  • drag a box to move it,
  • drag its corner dot to resize it,
  • click anywhere on the picture to jump the selected box there.

Auto-place and Spread out lay the boxes out for you. Nudging from there is quicker than placing each one from scratch.

4. Straighten first if the photo leans

If a shot is slightly tilted, fix it with the rotate slider under Alignment before you crop. A guide appears on the picture to line things up against. Rotating after cropping leaves empty corners, so the order matters.

5. Confirm and move on

When the boxes are where you want them, press Confirm & next (or Enter). Confirmed images get a green dot in the strip at the top, so you always know how far you are.

6. Output settings

  • Output size — Original pixels exports exactly the area you marked. Fixed size scales every crop to the same pixel dimensions (it needs a locked ratio).
  • Format and quality — WebP, JPEG or PNG. WebP is lightest for the web; PNG or a high-quality JPEG is the safe bet for print.
  • File names — the default {name}-d{n} gives shirt-d1.webp, shirt-d2.webp and so on. {nn} pads to 01, 02, and {w}×{h} adds the size.

7. Download the zip

Press Export all as .zip and every crop lands in your downloads as one zip. If you only need the image in front of you, use Export this image only.
