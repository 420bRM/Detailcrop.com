One thing to keep in mind: the ratio and output settings are set once for the whole batch; the box positions are set per photo. The rest is just following the steps below.

1. Load your images

Drop image files onto the left panel of the tool or press Choose images. JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and GIF work, up to 200 files and 200 MB each. Nothing leaves your computer, so there is no upload to wait for.

The tool opens with a sample image already loaded, so you can try things right away. The How it works button at the top walks you through the screen one step at a time.

2. Pick a ratio

Choose the crop ratio in the Settings panel under the picture: 1:1, 4:5, 2:3, 3:4, 5:7, 16:9 or 9:16, a ratio of your own under Custom, or Free for no ratio at all. With a ratio locked, every crop on every image comes out the same shape.

Sometimes one image needs more than one shape. Switch the toggle above the ratio buttons from Whole batch to Selected crop and only the box you have selected changes — handy for pulling a 4:5 feed post and a 16:9 banner out of the same photo.

3. Output size, format and file names

  • Output size — Original pixels exports exactly the area you marked. Fixed size scales every crop to the same pixel dimensions (it needs a locked ratio).
  • Format and quality — WebP, JPEG or PNG. WebP is lightest for the web; PNG or a high-quality JPEG is the safe bet for print.
  • File names — the default {name}-d{n} gives shirt-d1.webp, shirt-d2.webp and so on. {nn} pads to 01, 02, and {w}×{h} adds the size.

You can change these right before exporting, but if you want a fixed size, set it now: a box smaller than the output size gets a warning while you work, rather than after you have finished.

4. Straighten first if the photo leans

If a shot is slightly tilted, open Alignment on the right and fix it with the rotate slider before you place any boxes. A grid appears over the picture to line things up against. The horizontal and vertical sliders pull a keystoned wall or frame back to square.

Straightening trims the usable area a little, so it is easier to place the boxes afterwards.

5. Set the crop count and place the boxes

Choose how many crops this image gets on the right — up to six, or 0 to skip it. The count is per image, and each new image starts at one.

  • drag a box to move it,
  • drag its corner dot to resize it,
  • click anywhere on the picture to jump the selected box there.

Select a box by its number tag or with the 1–6 keys. Auto-place and Spread out lay the boxes out for you; nudging from there is quicker than placing each one from scratch.

6. Confirm and move on

When the boxes are where you want them, press Confirm & next (or Enter). Confirmed images get a green dot in the strip at the top, so you always know how far you are.

7. Download the zip

Press Export all as .zip and every crop lands in your downloads as one zip. If you only need the image in front of you, use Export this image only.
